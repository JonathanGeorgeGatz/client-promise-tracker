THE CLIENT PROMISE TRACKER — the exact prompts from the episode, word for word.
Paste them into your AI assistant in order. Prompt 1 builds the first version; prompts 2 and 3
are the fixes from the episode — they exist because the first two versions graded promises the
business never kept, and hid the ones it was breaking.

================================ PROMPT 1 ================================

Build me a single HTML file I can open in my browser, no internet, no account, that keeps track
of the promises my business makes to clients.

What it's for: I run a small remodeling studio. Every week we tell clients things — "I'll email
the revised price," "the installer will call Tuesday," "the samples go out Friday." Those
commitments live in two places: a client list and a promise log we keep as spreadsheets. Nobody
can say, on a given morning, what we owe people today, what we've already let slip, or which
client is quietly collecting broken promises.

Data in: two pastes.
1) The client list: CSV with columns Client, Contact, Email.
2) The promise log: CSV with columns Client, Promise, Made, Due, Owner, Status, Done, At Stake,
   Note — what was promised, when we said it, when it's due, who said it, whether it's open,
   done or cancelled, when it actually got done, and roughly what the job behind it is worth.

What I want out: the morning docket — everything overdue and everything due today, oldest
first; a per-client ledger with a kept-on-time rate; the dollars sitting behind the overdue
ones; a per-person table of who promised what; and a printable promise history per client, so
when someone says "you said Tuesday," the answer comes off a page instead of out of an
argument.

================================ PROMPT 2 ================================

The first version flattered us. Rebuild the bookkeeping:

- Kept is not a mood. A done promise is KEPT ON TIME or KEPT LATE, measured in working days
  against its due date — Monday to Friday, weekends don't count against anyone.
- A promise marked done with NO completion date is not evidence of anything. Refuse the row,
  out loud, with its row number.
- Matching is forgiving where it's safe: trim stray spaces, ignore case — a name typed from a
  phone in caps is still the same client. But a client who isn't on the client list at all is
  refused, not invented.
- Cancelled promises are listed separately, charged to nothing, and never counted as kept.
- Anything unreadable — a date that isn't a date, an amount that isn't an amount, a status that
  isn't open, done or cancelled — is refused with the reason. Never guessed at, never skipped
  in silence.

================================ PROMPT 3 ================================

Now make it trustworthy enough to settle the "you said Tuesday" argument.

- The as-of date is typed into the page. The machine clock gets no vote: the same two files
  must produce the same docket on any computer, on any day. A completion dated after the as-of
  hasn't happened yet at that reading — the promise is still open then, not kept.
- A promise whose Made date is after the as-of isn't on the books yet at that reading — list
  it, charge it to nothing.
- Every counted row lands in exactly ONE bucket: kept on time, kept late, overdue, due today,
  due soon, upcoming, not yet made, or cancelled. Due today is its own bucket — the day's own
  promises never go invisible.
- All money in whole cents, all rates in whole tenths of a percent, one written-down rounding
  rule (round half up). Grace is a setting in working days, not a kindness applied by hand.
- The client list refuses duplicates — the first row is kept, the copy is listed — so nobody's
  ledger gets counted twice.
- The page audits itself on every run: client rows in = clients + refused; promise rows in =
  the eight buckets + refused; and the board must re-total from the per-client ledgers to the
  row and to the cent. If any audit fails, or too many rows were refused, print DO NOT TRUST
  THIS DOCKET across the top, say why, and withhold every history — a wrong promise history is
  worse than none.

=========================== THE SECOND OPINION ===========================

The episode also used an independent checker — the same written rules implemented again in
Python by a separate session, sharing no code with the page — run on the same two files.
checker.py is in this folder. Two implementations, one spec: 209 paired runs over generated
studios compared 68,292 values and disagreed on none. When they do disagree, one of them is
wrong, and it is worth knowing which before a client calls.
